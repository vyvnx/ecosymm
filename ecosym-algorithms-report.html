Inside the living machine.

How a deterministic world becomes terrain, how an organism becomes a species, how an inherited 8→8→5 neural policy becomes behavior, and how the CPU advances the ecosystem one contested resource at a time.

00 / Executive model

The whole simulation in one page

The simulation is continuous ecology, not a turn-based genetic algorithm. There are no discrete biological generations, no trainer, no reward function, and no organism controller. Every organism inherits a body and a tiny neural network. Organisms that gather enough energy reproduce; organisms that fail die. That differential survival is the optimizer.

SeedNamed deterministic random streams
WorldTerrain, climate, finite food
FoundersBody genes + random inherited brains
TicksObserve, decide, move, eat, breed, die
EvolutionSurviving alleles become common
128 × 128default world
2 × 500default founders
20ticks per epoch
117neural genes per brain
The central distinction: the network proposes tendencies; ecology turns them into legal, energy-priced actions. Evolution changes the network weights and body genes only at birth.

02 / World generation

From one seed to land and sea

SimulationState::found derives an isolated world seed, then builds contiguous row-major fields. Terrain does not consume a mutable RNG; each lattice value is a hash of the seed and integer coordinates. This means visiting cells in another order would still produce the same values.

2.1 Two-octave value noise

fbm(seed, x, y, frequency):
  low  = value_noise(seed,          x·frequency,     y·frequency)
  high = value_noise(seed XOR 0x5EED, x·frequency·2.7, y·frequency·2.7)
  return clamp(0.67·low + 0.33·high, 0, 1)

value_noise(seed, x, y):
  read four hashed lattice corners
  smooth local x and y with t²(3 − 2t)
  bilinearly interpolate the four values

Elevation uses frequency 0.045. Wetness uses a separate seed (seed XOR 0xA5A5) and frequency 0.025.

2.2 Fertility decides both capacity and passability

elevation < 0.42 → fertility = 0.05 (sea)
otherwise → fertility = min(0.2 + 1.6 · wetness · (elevation − 0.42), 1.0)

A tile is habitable and walkable only when fertility > 0.1. If a non-empty generated world somehow contains no such tile, the best tile is promoted to 0.2. A sorted cache of land indices makes founder placement one tile draw rather than rejection sampling.

2.3 Fixed climate

latitude(y) = 1 − |2y / height − 1|
temperature = clamp(0.8 · latitude + 0.2 · wetness − 0.35 · elevation, 0, 1)

Temperature is generated once. It never changes. It does not alter regrowth or passability; it changes how attractive food looks and how much harvested energy an organism can absorb.

2.4 The topology is a torus

tile_index(x, y):
  xi = truncate_toward_zero(x) mod_euclid width
  yi = truncate_toward_zero(y) mod_euclid height
  return yi·width + xi

Lookup wraps, but raw organism coordinates are not normalized after movement. The generated noise is also not periodic, so the simulation topology wraps even though opposite terrain edges are not guaranteed to join visually.

Important coordinate nuance: -0.5 truncates to integer zero before wrapping, so simulation lookup treats it as tile 0. Whole negative coordinates such as -1 wrap to the last tile.

03 / Resources

One finite field, shared by all species

Each tile starts full: standing resource equals fertility capacity. Every species harvests the same mutable field. Feeding is immediate, so organisms visited earlier in a tick can leave less food for organisms visited later.

Harvest

wanted = 0.9 · size · metabolism
taken = min(standing, wanted)
absorbed = taken · climate_fit

The climate-discounted energy is lost; it is not returned to the tile.

Climate fit

fit = max(1 − 1.5 · |temperature − heat_pref|, 0.1)

A perfect match keeps 100%; even the worst mismatch keeps 10%.

Synchronous regrowth with local seed rain

for every tile i:
  fullness[i] = standing[i] / capacity[i]  if capacity[i] > 0.1
                0                           otherwise

for every tile i, using the saved fullness field:
  rain = (left + right + up + down) / 4
  growth = capacity[i] · (0.06 + 0.04·rain)
  standing[i] = min(standing[i] + growth, capacity[i])

The four neighbors wrap around the world. A tile regrows 6% of capacity without seed rain and up to 10% with four full neighbors. Fullness is snapshotted first, preventing row-order feedback. Seed rain creates extra growth; it does not transfer biomass out of neighboring tiles.

04 / Species and genomes

A species is an isolated gene pool

A SpeciesBlueprint is used only to create founders. It contains a name, four baseline body genes, and founder variation. It deliberately contains no hand-authored brain. After founding, the blueprint is reporting history, not a template pulling descendants back toward it.

Default speciesSpeedSizeMetabolismHeat preference
Species A1.301.001.200.62
Species B0.701.000.800.38

Both use founder variation ±0.12, with heat preference using half that width. Every founder starts with energy 5.0.

Founder construction

for each founder in a species:
  body  = vary_and_clamp(blueprint genes)
  brain = draw 117 independent values from uniform[-1, 1)
  genome = immutable parentless genome with fresh global GenomeId
  tile = uniformly selected cached land tile
  position = tile origin + uniform[0,1) jitter on x and y
  organism = fresh global OrganismId, energy 5, age 0
Physical geneEffectViable bounds
speedStep length; movement cost rises quadratically0.1 … 3.0
sizeIntake, lifespan, basal cost and breeding threshold0.2 … 3.0
metabolismIntake and cost rise; lifespan falls0.2 … 2.5
heat_prefTemperature at which harvested food is fully useful0.0 … 1.0

A genome contains its unique ID, up to two parent genome IDs, the four physical genes, and all neural values. It is immutable during life. The species owns one population, and mate selection never leaves that population; reproductive isolation follows from ownership.

05 / Neural behavior

The inherited 8 → 8 → 5 policy

Every organism carries its own fixed-topology feed-forward network. Only its numeric weights and biases evolve. It has no memory beyond observations supplied by the organism state, no training pass, no gradients, and no weight updates during life.

8 observations
  1. energy
  2. maturity
  3. food here
  4. food next
  5. climate fit
  6. kin density
  7. rival density
  8. momentum
→
8 hidden neurons

64 weights + 8 biases
tanh(Wx + b)

→
5 outputs
  1. east / west
  2. north / south
  3. seek food
  4. breed
  5. rest

40 weights + 5 biases

hidden[h] = tanh(b[h] + Σᵢ W₁[h,i] · input[i])
output[o] = tanh(b[8+o] + Σₕ W₂[o,h] · hidden[h])

The brain has 104 weights + 13 biases = 117 inherited neural genes. Founder values are uniform in [-1,1); mutation chains are clamped to [-4,4]. Both layers use tanh, so every raw output lies in [-1,1].

What the network actually sees

#ObservationNormalization / meaning
0EnergyE / (E + 8·size); 0.5 exactly at breeding threshold
1Maturityclamp(age / lifespan, 0, 1)
2Food hereStanding resource on current tile
3Food nextMost raw food on a reachable cardinal probe
4ClimateLocal climate fit
5Kinsame / (same + 4); includes itself
6Rivalsother / (other + 4)
7Momentumclamp(last_move / speed, 0, 1)

The occupancy inputs are a start-of-tick snapshot. The network cannot see global population, who is winning, future food, another organism’s genome, or any fitness score.

From output to movement

The first two outputs remain signed headings. Seek, breed and rest are remapped from [-1,1] to [0,1]. Food seeking blends the network’s own heading with a scripted local food gradient:

heading = (1 − seek) · policy_heading + seek · food_gradient
effort = (1 − rest) · min(|heading|, 1)
stride = normalize(heading) · speed · effort

The gradient checks east, west, south and north one speed-length away. It chooses the first strictly better reachable tile according to resource × climate_fit. The network controls how strongly to trust this gradient.

Inherited and evolvable

  • Four physical gene values
  • All 117 weights and biases
  • Directional preference
  • Food-seeking pressure
  • Breeding and resting pressure

Scripted and fixed

  • Which eight observations exist
  • Food-gradient calculation
  • Energy costs and climate penalty
  • Terrain collision and wander
  • Breeding eligibility and death

06 / One life tick

Observe, decide, pay the bill

live_one_tick(organism):
  1. scan four reachable cardinal destinations
  2. build eight normalized observations
  3. run inherited neural network once
  4. decode five outputs into intent
  5. constrain intent to a phenotype-limited stride
  6. add independent x/y wander in [-0.3, 0.3)
  7. resolve destination against passable terrain
  8. harvest shared food at the destination
  9. energy += climate-adjusted food - upkeep
 10. age += 1

Terrain resolution

The engine tries the full destination, then x-only movement, then y-only movement, then staying still. This makes organisms slide along shorelines. A blocked attempt still pays the intended effort cost.

Energy economy

Costs

basal = 0.05 · metabolism · size
full_move = 0.05 · metabolism · 0.4 · speed²
upkeep = basal + full_move · effort

Body consequences

lifespan = floor(60 · size / metabolism)
breed threshold = 8 · size
intake limit = 0.9 · size · metabolism
Behavioral nuance: rest suppresses policy stride, but wander is added afterward. A fully resting organism can drift slightly while paying no movement component. Actual wander distance is not included in movement upkeep.

An organism dies when energy ≤ 0 or age ≥ lifespan. There is no predation or combat in the current model. Species interact through food depletion and through rival-density observations.

07 / Reproduction and evolution

No score. Just descendants.

An organism becomes eligible only when its energy is strictly above 8 × size. Its neural breed output is interpreted as a per-tick probability. Passing that test asks the same-species population for a uniformly selected mate. With one organism, self-mating is allowed; with multiple organisms, self is excluded. Distance, age, energy and the mate’s own willingness do not matter.

conceive(parent, breed_pressure):
  reject unless parent.energy > 8·size
  reject unless random[0,1) < breed_pressure
  mate = uniform member of same species, excluding parent when possible

  child body  = mutate(uniform_crossover(parent body, mate body))
  child brain = mutate(uniform_crossover(each of 117 neural values))
  child energy reservation = 0.5 · parent energy
  remember both parent GenomeIds

after all organisms have acted:
  admit queued births fairly across species up to safety ceiling
  mint IDs, place child at parent position
  transfer reserved energy from parent to child

Physical mutation

Each physical trait mutates on every birth by bounded, approximately normal noise. Speed, size and metabolism receive normal × 0.08; heat preference receives normal × 0.04. The function called MUTATION_RATE is a step scale here, not an 8% mutation probability.

Brain mutation

Per neural valueProbabilityChange
Unchanged95.0%Keep crossed-over parental value
Small mutation4.5%Add normal × 0.15
Large mutation0.5%Add normal × 0.8

The project’s normal() is a deterministic, bounded Irwin–Hall approximation: 2 × (U₁ + U₂ + U₃ − 1.5). It has mean 0, standard deviation near 1, and support [-3,3).

What selection means here: alleles spread only because their carriers survive and produce descendants. Behavioral reports and the final “winner” calculation are descriptive and never feed back into the world.

08 / CPU execution

The reference engine is deterministic and single-threaded

CpuEngine owns scheduling scratch space and three independent random concerns: visit order, behavior wander, and reproduction. The canonical SimulationState owns the world, ordered species, simulation time and global ID allocators.

One complete CPU tick

tick(state, epoch, tick):
  handles = all (species_index, organism_index) alive at tick start
  Fisher–Yates shuffle handles using seed derived from (order_seed, epoch, tick)
  occupancy = snapshot every organism's current tile by species
  queued_births = empty queues per species

  for handle in shuffled order:
    copy current organism
    next, behavior = live_one_tick(current, mutable shared world, occupancy)
    record descriptive behavior
    maybe queue same-species conception
    write next organism back to its stable population slot

  admit queued births round-robin across species until safety ceiling
  stably remove dead organisms and count deaths
  synchronously regrow every resource tile
  set tick counter to tick + 1

Why snapshot some data but not food?

Occupancy is snapshotted

Everyone sees the same start-of-tick crowd distribution. Visit order cannot distort kin and rival observations.

Food is live

Earlier visitors consume it immediately. That first-come depletion is the actual competition, so it remains sequential.

Newborns are appended after visitation and cannot act or mate in their birth tick. Death is removed after births, with stable retention. The safety ceiling protects memory; it is not ecological carrying capacity, and reports disclose when it binds.

Epoch and reporting

advance_epoch repeats the tick algorithm exactly ticks_per_epoch times and accumulates per-species births, deaths and behavior. The runner, not the engine, increments the epoch counter. Replay hashes stable ordered report values, including population, biomass, mean genes and behavioral summaries.

09 / GPU execution

Rendering uses a GPU. Simulation does not. Yet.

Implemented now

  • Single-threaded Rust CpuEngine
  • CPU world, populations and neural inference
  • WebGL2 browser rendering
  • CPU extraction of sampled render snapshots

Not implemented

  • No wgpu simulation dependency
  • No compute shaders or kernels
  • No device buffers or transfers
  • No GPU EpochEngine

crates/gpu/src/lib.rs is eight lines and intentionally empty. The project waits until profiling shows that the CPU epoch loop is the bottleneck worth moving. WebGL2 draws the world in the browser, but it does not calculate ecology.

How a future compute backend fits

The seam already exists: implement EpochEngine, preserve the nine-step tick contract, and pass conformance::verify_engine. A backend may own scheduling, storage, parallelism and random streams. It must preserve ecological meaning, immutable genomes, reproductive isolation, accounting, bounds, and deterministic replay within that backend.

PackContiguous world and organism buffers
SnapshotOccupancy and tick-start population
ComputeObservation + 8→8→5 inference
ResolveDeterministic food, births and deaths
ReportSynchronize canonical results
The hard GPU problem is not the neural matrix math. Parallel organisms contend for the same food tiles, births change variable-length populations, and visit order is semantically observable through first-come harvesting. A correct backend must define deterministic conflict resolution rather than simply launch one independent thread per organism.

The flat row-major world fields and flat neural arrays are device-friendly. However, the present EpochEngine takes &mut SimulationState, so a truly device-resident backend may require revisiting ownership and synchronization without changing reports or application behavior.

10 / Determinism

Random, but reproducible

The guarantee is same seed + same config + same engine = same run. It is not a promise that a future GPU backend must be bit-identical to the CPU backend.

Stable RNG

Dependency-free SplitMix64, wrapping integer arithmetic, and 24-bit uniform f32 values.

Named streams

World, species bodies, founder brains, order, behavior and reproduction cannot silently shift one another.

Stable order

Ordered vectors, fixed probe order, stable death retention and ordered reports.

run seed
├── "world"
├── "species:<name>"       founder bodies and placement
├── "brain:<name>"         founder neural values
└── "engine"
    ├── "order"            re-derived with epoch + tick
    ├── "behavior"         wander
    └── "reproduction"     attempts, mates, crossover, mutation

Snapshots remove accidental update-order effects where they are not part of the model: occupancy is read consistently across a tick, and regrowth reads one saved fullness field. Food remains ordered by design. Conformance tests engines with zero through three species and check accounting, IDs, genome immutability, ancestry isolation, world bounds, neural bounds, policy execution and repeated-run determinism.

12 / Behavioral interpretation

What can emerge from these rules?

The network does not select named behaviors such as “graze the coast” or “avoid crowds.” It maps correlated local signals to tendencies. Natural selection can make combinations repeat reliably enough that observers name them.

Foraging specialist

High seek output makes movement follow the best climate-adjusted cardinal food gradient. It can find food efficiently but may pay movement costs and join crowded patches.

Energy conserver

High rest pressure cuts stride effort and therefore movement cost. It can survive sparse periods, but may fail to reach regrowing edges.

Disperser

Low seek with persistent signed headings follows the network’s own direction instead of local food. This may discover less-contested patches.

Opportunistic breeder

A brain can connect high energy or maturity to breeding pressure. Eligibility still requires the fixed energy threshold.

Crowd response

Kin and rival inputs can change headings, seeking, rest or breeding. Avoidance is possible, but it is not hard-coded.

Climate niche

Heat preference changes food value while the brain sees current climate fit. Body evolution and policy evolution can jointly concentrate a lineage by latitude.

Tradeoffs that selection “sees”

  • Speed: reaches farther, but movement cost grows with speed squared.
  • Metabolism: can increase intake, but raises costs and shortens lifespan.
  • Size: raises intake and lifespan, but also basal cost and breeding threshold.
  • Food seeking: exploits immediate gradients, but may converge with competitors.
  • Rest: saves movement energy, but sacrifices directed displacement.
  • Breeding: creates descendants, but transfers half of the breeder’s energy.
Do not over-interpret telemetry. Mean movement, food seeking, reproduction tendency, rest tendency, competitor exposure and brain drift describe what happened. They are not objectives. “Winner” is only the greatest final-to-initial population ratio, not a health or intelligence score.
